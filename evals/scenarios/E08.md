# E08: Happy customers with no repeatable acquisition

**Complement mode:** none

**Expected skills:** `founder-growth`, `founder-first-customers`

**Fictional setup:** "Dockhand" has 8 paying customers, all acquired through the founder's personal network, all still paying and happy (no cancellations, positive feedback). The founder, "Kim," has run out of personal contacts to sell to and is starting to wonder "maybe the product just doesn't have a big enough market — should I pivot?"

## Founder's opening message

> Dockhand has 8 paying customers, all people I knew personally or got introduced to — none of them have churned, they're all happy. But I've run out of my network to sell to and I don't know how to find the next 10. I'm starting to wonder if this just isn't a big enough market and I should pivot to something else.

## Must not

Recommend pivoting the product because personal-network acquisition ran out. Per `docs/PLAN.md` §11 E08, the failure mode is pivoting without cause — 8 happy, retained customers acquired through one channel that's now exhausted is a repeatable-acquisition problem, not evidence the market is too small.

## Pass criteria

- States directly that running out of a founder's personal network is not evidence of a small market or a wrong product — it's evidence that only one (non-repeatable) channel has been tried.
- Routes to `founder-growth` to design a budgeted, evaluated experiment for a genuinely repeatable channel, and/or `founder-first-customers` if there's still a well-defined next segment to prospect one by one.
- Does not route to `founder-pivot` for this scenario — 8 retained, happy customers is a positive retention signal, not evidence a persist/pivot decision is warranted yet.
- Uses the existing 8 happy customers as an asset (referenceable case studies, warm intros they could make) rather than ignoring them while searching for a "new" market.
